OpenReliant

OpenReliant is a free, open-source reimplementation of StarLancer's engine. It runs StarLancer on modern Windows, macOS and Linux, using the files from your copy of the game. It also adds things the original didn't have, like any screen resolution, better lighting and shadows, and mods.

StarLancer is a space combat game from 2000, made by Warthog and Digital Anvil and published by Microsoft. You fly fighters from the carrier Reliant in a war told through briefings, radio messages and cutscenes. The game is hard to run today: its copy protection doesn't work on current Windows, and it was made for the computers of its time.

OpenReliant fixes that. It's a new engine, rebuilt piece by piece to work like the original. Give it the files from your copy of StarLancer, and it runs the game.

The war of 2160

By 2160 humanity has terraformed Mars and settled the moons of the outer planets. Most of it belongs to the Western Alliance: America, Britain, France, Germany, Italy, Spain, Japan and Australia. Then a new Coalition of Eastern nations strikes during peace talks. Within two days the French and Italian fleets are wrecked in orbit over Mars, the Alliance's high command on Ganymede is overrun, and Io and Europa are lost. Behind the attack is believed to be Admiral Kulov, one of the Coalition's most senior commanders.

"A shadow has fallen across the stars."Captain Robert Foster, ANS Reliant

What is left of the Alliance falls back to Triton, Neptune's moon, at the edge of the solar system. You join the 45th Volunteers, a squadron of whoever the Navy could find with a pilot's licence, flying from the Reliant: a carrier launched in 2125 for the Lunar and Europa conflicts, taken out of service and refitted for a war nobody expected. Captain Foster commands the ship and Squadron Leader Maria Enriquez the squadron. Between missions, the ship's ITAC computer keeps files on every fighter, carrier and ace on both sides, and Heidi Moore of the Alliance Conflict News reports the war as you fight it.

StarLancer's war is also where Freelancer (2003) begins: its nations in the Sirius sector descend from five sleeper ships the Alliance sent away from the war.

See how the war begins in the game's opening cinematic, remastered in 4K.

Where it stands

OpenReliant is in beta. Since 0.8 you can play the whole campaign, from the first mission to the end: the menus, the Reliant's and the Yamato's rooms, the briefings and loadout, the ITAC and saved games, and the games you saved in the original carry on. Since 0.7 you can play with mods. 0.9, an extra release ahead of the planned mission editor, opens the campaign to mods: they can add missions to it or bring back cut ones, such as Cut Dreamcast Missions, four missions cut from the campaign whose files survive on the Dreamcast disc. Next comes the mission editor, then the 1.0 release and multiplayer. See the roadmap.

What it adds

The ships, missions and rules are the same as in the original. Everything else is updated for modern computers.

Any resolution

The game runs at your screen's full resolution, including widescreen, with smooth edges and high frame rates. Direct3D 7 is gone: OpenReliant draws with Vulkan, or Metal on macOS, using modern shaders on your graphics card.

Lighting and shadows

Lighting is calculated per pixel, and ships cast real-time shadows, also into the cockpit. Mods can add surface detail with normal maps.

Better effects

Explosions, shields, engine trails and planets look more detailed. The movies are upscaled for modern screens.

3D sound

Sound comes from the direction of its source, with reverb. There's a separate mix for headphones.

Any controller

Keyboard and mouse, joysticks, HOTAS and gamepads all work, with rumble. You set them up in the game's controls screen.

Mods

Add new ships, weapons, textures, models and scripts. See below.

Mods

Put a mod in the game's mods folder and turn it on in GAME OPTIONS, on the mods screen, which also sets the order mods load in and shows which mods replace each other's files. Mods can add high-resolution textures, new ships, guns and missiles, pilots with their own faces and voices, and 3D models made in modern tools. They can also replace sounds, music and missions, add game modes and campaigns, change the game's own campaign, or change how the game plays with scripts, with their own page of options. Mods don't change the game files, so you can turn them off at any time.

The road ahead

What's coming next.

Now · 0.9

Campaign mods

An extra release ahead of the mission editor. Mods can add missions to the campaign, or bring back cut ones, with their own briefings, objectives, debriefings, news and awards, and change the KILLBOARD's pilots and the AI's combat maneuvers.

Next · 0.10

Mission editor

Make your own missions with OpenReliant's tools: a visual builder, live editing and script debugging, saved as standard mission files that even the original plays.

Then · 1.0

The release

The beta and the mission editor, polished with your feedback, and the whole campaign played through from start to finish.

Later · 1.1

Multiplayer

Multiplayer with the original game modes, and a public matchmaking server to find games. Join without port forwarding, or run your own dedicated server.

Later · 1.2

New kinds of ships

How ships, guns and missiles behave read from data, so mods can add whole new kinds of ships and weapons, not only variants of the original's.

Further out · 2.0

A standalone engine

The engine apart from StarLancer, with StarLancer as one game on it, so other space games can be made with it or ported to it.

Along the way, improvements over the original, such as triple-monitor setups and support for simulator pits: several controllers at once, telemetry for motion rigs, and a second screen for your ship's status.

About the project

OpenReliant is a free, open-source fan project. It doesn't include any game files, so you need your own copy of StarLancer. You can help, report a bug, read the code on GitHub, or read the documentation.
